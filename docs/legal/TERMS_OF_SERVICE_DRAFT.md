# Terms of service: draft

> **Draft 0.1, 22 November 2026. Not in effect. Not published. Not reviewed by a lawyer. Not
> approved by the owner. Not legal advice.** Nobody can create an account today, so there is nothing
> for these terms to govern yet. They must not be published until the service exists, every
> placeholder is filled, a lawyer has reviewed them under the law of each country where Zavolt is
> offered, and the owner has approved them. Publishing them is a change to website text, which is a
> code change.

## How to read this draft

**Part A** is the draft text. **Part B** explains the legal constraints behind it, with sources.
Markers: **[PLACEHOLDER: …]** (a fact only the owner can supply), **[DECISION: …]** (an open
choice), **[LEGAL REVIEW: …]** (a question for a lawyer), **[ONLY WHEN TRUE: …]** (text allowed only
once the feature exists and is verified).

The draft avoids two things on purpose: blanket exclusions of liability, and any suggestion that
users give up rights the law gives them. Consumer protection rules forbid both (Part B).

## Part B: notes for the owner and the lawyer

Labels: **Requirement** (a legal rule, with its source), **Practice**, **Decision**, **Question**.

### B1. Unfair terms

**Requirement.** A term that was not individually negotiated "shall be regarded as unfair if,
contrary to the requirement of good faith, it causes a significant imbalance in the parties' rights
and obligations arising under the contract, to the detriment of the consumer" (Directive 93/13/EEC,
Art. 3(1)). The Annex lists terms that may be unfair, including terms:

- (1)(a) "excluding or limiting the legal liability of a seller or supplier in the event of the
  death of a consumer or personal injury";
- (1)(b) "inappropriately excluding or limiting the legal rights of the consumer" for
  non-performance or inadequate performance;
- (1)(j) "enabling the seller or supplier to alter the terms of the contract unilaterally without a
  valid reason which is specified in the contract".

Sections 16 and 19 are drafted with these in mind. **Question:** Romania's transposing law (Law no.
193/2000) has not been reviewed.

### B2. Digital content and digital services

- **Requirement.** Directive (EU) 2019/770 applies where a consumer pays a price, and also where the
  consumer provides personal data instead, "except where the personal data provided by the consumer
  are exclusively processed by the trader for the purpose of supplying the digital content or
  digital service" or for legal requirements (Art. 3(1)). **Question:** whether a free tier would
  fall within it.
- **Requirement.** Terms that exclude, derogate from or vary the national rules transposing the
  Directive to the consumer's detriment are not binding (Art. 22(1)).
- **Requirement.** After termination the trader must not use content the consumer provided or
  created, apart from listed exceptions (Art. 16(3)), and must, at the consumer's request, make that
  content available "free of charge, without hindrance from the trader, within a reasonable time and
  in a commonly used and machine-readable format" (Art. 16(4)). Personal data is governed by the
  GDPR (Art. 16(2)). Section 10 follows this.

### B3. Right of withdrawal

- **Requirement.** Consumers have 14 days to withdraw from a distance contract without giving
  reasons (Directive 2011/83/EU, Art. 9(1)). The exceptions include service contracts "after the
  service has been fully performed", subject to the consumer's prior express consent and
  acknowledgement (Art. 16(a)), and digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, subject to
  conditions (Art. 16(m)).
- **Question.** How these apply to a subscription to an ongoing digital service, what the consumer
  owes if they ask for the service to start during the withdrawal period, and Romania's transposing
  law (Emergency Ordinance no. 34/2014, not reviewed).

### B4. Changes to the service

**Requirement.** Under Directive (EU) 2019/770, Art. 19, a trader may modify a digital service
supplied over a period beyond what keeps it in conformity only if the contract allows it and gives a
valid reason, the change costs the consumer nothing, and the consumer is told clearly. Where the
change negatively affects access or use more than minorly, the consumer must be told in advance on a
durable medium and may terminate free of charge within 30 days. Section 11 points to this.

### B5. Applicable law and courts

- **Requirement.** A choice of law in a consumer contract "may not, however, have the result of
  depriving the consumer of the protection afforded to him by provisions that cannot be derogated
  from by agreement" under the law of the consumer's habitual residence (Regulation (EC) No
  593/2008, Art. 6(2)).
- **Requirement.** A consumer may sue in the courts of their own domicile, and the trader may sue a
  consumer only there (Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012, Art. 18(1) and (2)).
- **Requirement.** The EU's online dispute resolution platform was discontinued and Regulation (EU)
  No 524/2013 repealed from 20 July 2025 (Regulation (EU) 2024/3228, Arts 1 and 2), so the terms
  must not link to it. **Question:** which Romanian or other alternative dispute resolution body
  applies, and what information about it is required.

### B6. Rights in the software that cannot be excluded

**Requirement.** Contract terms contrary to the right to decompile for interoperability (Art. 6), or
to the back-up and the observe-study-test exceptions (Art. 5(2) and (3)), are null and void
(Directive 2009/24/EC, Art. 8). Section 7 says so.

### B7. Information about the operator

**Requirement.** An online service must make its operator's name, geographic address, email address
and any trade register and number easily, directly and permanently accessible (Directive 2000/31/EC,
Art. 5(1)). Directive 2011/83/EU, Art. 6(1), lists the pre-contract information for consumers. Both
need the facts in `LEGAL_OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

### B8. The Digital Services Act

**Question.** The Digital Services Act defines a "hosting" service as one "consisting of the storage
of information provided by, and at the request of, a recipient of the service" (Regulation (EU)
2022/2065, Art. 3(g)(iii)). A password vault may fall within that definition. If it does, Zavolt
would need points of contact (Arts 11 and 12), information about any restrictions on use in its
terms (Art. 14(1)), a way for anyone to notify illegal content (Art. 16(1)), and statements of
reasons for restrictions (Art. 17(1)). Some duties do not apply to micro and small enterprises (for
example Art. 15(2)). Zavolt is not an "online platform" as long as it does not disseminate
information to the public (Art. 3(i)). How notice and action works when the provider cannot read the
content is a question for a lawyer. The Regulation has applied since 17 February 2024 (Art. 93(2)).

### B9. The Cyber Resilience Act

**Question with a deadline.** Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 lists "Password managers" among the
important products with digital elements in Class I of Annex III. It applies from 11 December 2027,
but its reporting duties for actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents (Art. 14) apply
from 11 September 2026, to products made available on the market (Art. 71(2); Art. 69 for products
placed on the market earlier). Its rules include a support period that "shall be at least five
years" unless the product is expected to be used for less (Art. 13), and a conformity assessment
route that, for Class I products, depends on whether harmonised standards are applied (Art. 32).
What this means for a downloadable extension and desktop application, and for the service behind
them, needs a lawyer's assessment before release. Section 11's support period depends on it.

### B10. Language and form

**Question.** Whether the terms must be available in Romanian, and in the languages of other
countries where Zavolt is offered, and which version prevails.
